DNS Best Practices

DNS is more than name resolution. In cloud architecture, DNS influences service discovery, private access, failover, and how traffic is routed among regions and applications.

Home LB & DNS Deep dives

Key principles

  • Use private DNS for internal-only workloads and service discovery.
  • Keep public and private namespaces clearly separated to avoid confusion.
  • Align DNS records with your load-balancer and failover design.

Why it matters

Choosing the wrong DNS pattern can point clients to the wrong region, create split-horizon confusion, or break internal service communication. In network design exams, DNS is often treated as part of the availability and routing strategy.

Design checklist

  • Use health checks and weighted routing when designing resiliency.
  • Decide which domains are public vs private.
  • Ensure internal apps do not depend on public DNS when private connectivity is required.

What to remember

Memory hook

Private DNS keeps internal apps private; public DNS informs the internet. The right answer often depends on whether the client is internal or external.

  • Use split-horizon patterns when the same service name has different internal and external answers.
  • DNS and load balancing must align to avoid traffic being sent to a dead or wrong region.
  • Remember: traffic direction and naming strategy are part of the design decision.
